Welcome to this specialised Master-level course in Enterprise Architecture, designed for students in Business Informatics.
This course will equip you with a profound understanding of Enterprise Architecture (EA) and its critical application in solving complex organisational challenges.
Our primary focus will be on achieving **strategic alignment**, fostering **enterprise coherence**, and effectively **governing value creation**, particularly within the dynamic context of **strategic planning** and the **implementation of digital transformation** initiatives.

### Core Concepts of Enterprise Architecture

At its heart, Enterprise Architecture provides a holistic framework for understanding, designing, and evolving an organisation.
It acts as a comprehensive blueprint, ensuring that an enterprise's business processes, information systems, and technology infrastructure are strategically aligned and work cohesively towards achieving its overarching goals.
We begin by establishing the **conceptual and theoretical framework** of Enterprise Architecture, exploring its foundational principles and the academic theories underpinning its practice.

A vital aspect of Enterprise Architecture is its ability to model and visualise an organisation.
For this, we will delve into **the ArchiMate language**, which is an open, independent modelling language standard for Enterprise Architecture.
ArchiMate enables us to describe, analyse, and visualise relationships between various architectural domains in a consistent and comprehensible manner.
To practically apply this language, we will utilise **the Archi tool**, an open-source modelling application specifically designed to support the ArchiMate language.
Through the use of Archi and ArchiMate, you will learn to create coherent architectural models that communicate complex enterprise structures and transformations effectively.

Practical application is paramount in Enterprise Architecture, which is why we will extensively analyse real-world scenarios through **case studies** such as **ArchiSurance** and **ArchiMetal**.
These cases provide opportunities to apply theoretical knowledge and modelling techniques to understand how EA addresses genuine business challenges.

Beyond understanding the current state and designing future states, Enterprise Architecture is intrinsically linked to an organisation's strategic capabilities.
We will explore **Capability-Based Management (CBM)**, a strategic planning approach that focuses on identifying, planning, and developing the business capabilities an organisation needs to execute its strategy.
EA plays a crucial role here by providing a framework to model and manage these capabilities, ensuring that supporting resources like processes, applications, and technology are aligned.

Enterprise Architecture is typically structured into several interconnected domains, providing a layered view of the enterprise.
**Business Architecture** focuses on the "what" and "why" of the business, including strategy, governance, organisation, and core business processes and capabilities.
It describes how an organisation achieves its objectives and delivers value to its stakeholders.
Moving down the architectural stack, **Data Architecture** defines the organisation's data assets, governing how data is stored, managed, and consumed.
**Application Architecture** details the individual applications and their interrelationships, ensuring they effectively support business processes and data flows.
Finally, **Technology Architecture** underpins everything, defining the hardware, software, network, and infrastructure components that host the applications and data.
Understanding these distinct yet interdependent architectural layers is critical for designing a robust and adaptable enterprise.

The journey of architectural transformation follows a structured approach, starting with the development of an **Architecture Vision**.
This vision defines the target future state of the enterprise, outlining the key business and technological changes required to achieve strategic objectives.
Following the vision, an **Architecture Roadmap** is developed, which is a detailed plan outlining the sequence of projects and activities needed to transition from the current state to the desired future state.
Ultimately, the course covers the complexities of **implementing digital transformation strategies**, demonstrating how EA provides the necessary guidance and governance to manage these extensive changes effectively.

We will also explore the vital chain that links **strategy to business model, to operating model, and ultimately to enterprise architecture**.
Strategy sets the direction, the business model defines how value is created and delivered, and the operating model describes *how* the organisation operates to deliver that value.
Enterprise Architecture then provides the detailed blueprint for the resources (people, processes, technology, information) required to implement the operating model, ensuring everything is aligned with the overall strategy.

To provide real-world insights, the course incorporates **guest lectures** from highly recognised consultancy firms or their clients.
These sessions offer invaluable perspectives on **Enterprise Architecture in practice**, showcasing how leading organisations apply EA principles to address contemporary business challenges.
Furthermore, we will touch upon current **Enterprise Architecture research**, including the emerging use of Large Language Models (LLMs) and other assistive tools for modelling, analysing, and designing enterprise Architecture, reflecting the innovative edge of the discipline.

### Examination Methods

Your understanding and application of the course material will be assessed through a combination of methods.
The **end-of-term assessment** comprises a written exam with multiple-choice questions and one open question relating to the guest lectures.
The multiple-choice questions are designed to evaluate your comprehension of the learning materials, your ability to apply these materials to various situations, and your capacity to analyse scenarios using the course concepts.
This is a limited open-book exam, allowing access to a pre-defined list of learning materials.
Additionally, **continuous assessment** involves evaluating the results of a group assignment, which will assess your ability to create and evaluate practical solutions using the knowledge and tools acquired throughout the course.

### Learning Materials

The learning materials for this course are diverse, combining theoretical foundations with practical application.
Core lectures will be supported by **PowerPoint presentations** and comprehensive **PDF documents**.
Crucially, you will engage directly with the **ArchiMate 3.2 specification** (and some aspects of ArchiMate 4 specification), which is the definitive guide to the modelling language.
Key academic papers will provide the intellectual depth for our discussions, including foundational work on **strategic alignment** by Henderson and Venkatraman (1999), insights into **digital transformation strategies** by Matt, Hess, & Benlian (2015) and Vial (2019), and discussions on navigating such transformations by Sebastian et al. (2020), Soh, Yeow & Goh (2023), and Wessel et al. (2021).
We will also draw from works like Ross, Weill, & Robertson's (2006) perspectives on **enterprise architecture as strategy** and Roelens, Steenacker, & Poels' (2019) research on **realising strategic fit within business architecture**.
Practical case studies, such as the **ArchiSurance case study** and the **ArchiMetal case study**, along with relevant sections from the **TOGAF 10 Reference Cards**, will further enhance your practical understanding.
Guest lecture materials, including Jonas Van Riel's (2025) work on **Capability-Based Management**, will provide contemporary industry insights.
All materials related to exercises and solutions, including the ArchiMate models, will be accessible via the Archi tool.
